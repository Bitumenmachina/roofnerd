# Sweep: a residential electrician (master, estimates his own work) on a renovation

Method: 31 pictures read in `plumbline/demos/captured/` (trades/3-electrical, 4-plumbing, 5-hvac, first-floor-alone; house/01, 05-sheet, 07-sheet, 08-sheet; layers/02, 03, 05; glass/05, 08; d2/04, 07; price/04 model and sheet; clash/04, 05, 06; field/03; renow/05, 09, 10; scanw/08; cabinetsw/08; layoutsw/06; foundw/05; money/04-sheet; plan/05), plus the frozen command line on a made-up `sixteen` house (`electrician/h.json`: estimate, show, takeoff, clashes, vif, items, check), plus research/trades-use-mep.md and judgment-reno-and-receptacles.md. The live window was not run. No picture shows the sheet's "Electrical 25" tab (not seen). No picture shows a switch, light or panel drawn as a device in 3D (not seen; the only electrical picture is trades/3-electrical.png).

## 1. Who I am and why I open this
1. Service and panel: what I have, what amps, how many spaces are open, and whether the new range, bath heat and kitchen counters fit. Do I need a service upgrade? That is the biggest line on the job.
2. Where cable runs and what it crosses: bored joists, a wall coming down (a live circuit in it must be capped, rerouted or removed first), ducts and drains in my way, fire blocking, attic.
3. Devices by room, existing against new: receptacle spacing, kitchen and bath circuits, GFCI and AFCI, smokes, so I know what the inspector counts.
4. My takeoff: boxes, devices by type, feet of each cable by gauge, breakers and panel spaces, and what is labor I can do at my own rate.

## 3. Missing or wrong for me
- Panel capacity: looked for amps, spaces, bus, used spaces, a load calc. Got "Panelboard, 200 A" as a name; the earlier review found `set ... amps` refused. Why it matters: a service upgrade or a subpanel is the single largest change order on a renovation.
- Circuits: looked for circuit number, breaker size, wire gauge, load, AFCI/GFCI, room. Got "kitchen circuit" as a cable run. No breaker lines, no panel schedule, no per-circuit load. A failed inspection (kitchen small-appliance circuits, bath circuit, dedicated circuits).
- Wire type: "0.4 in round" is not a cable. I buy 14/2, 12/2, 12/3, 10/3, 6/3, 2-2-2-4 SE, THHN in conduit. Pricing at one $/LF for all of it is wrong by 3x to 20x per run.
- Devices: starter library has one electrical item (Panelboard 200 A) plus a meter, duplex receptacle and ceiling light in the sample. No switch (single, 3-way, 4-way, dimmer), GFCI, AFCI, smoke/CO, recessed can, fan, disconnect, subpanel, junction/box types. A takeoff with no device counts is not mine.
- Boxes, covers, connectors, staples, wire nuts, rough/trim split: none; `fitting` refuses cable, so electrical fittings are zero (earlier review). The industry labor unit (hours per rough/trim) is absent; every line is "Subcontract".
- Counts: room receptacle count is a floor under 210.52(A) only, for habitable rooms. It prints 3 for a bath (a bath needs one GFCI at the basin, none by wall spacing), it counts nothing for a kitchen counter, and is not a priced line. It misleads on bath and kitchen. Plus nothing counts lights, switches, smokes by room, nor says where each goes.
- Existing vs new: demolished wall with cable inside it - no word. "Power 61 LF includes a cable I marked to demolish" (earlier review). I need "existing to remain", "existing to remove", "existing to extend", "new" as separate counts and lengths, and a "tie in here" point.
- Cable run length: runs from panel to device in straight plan segments plus rises. No drop to the box, no home run, no service loop, no allowance for the 6 in. of free conductor in each box, no fish through the wall in a finished wall, no attic vs wall vs basement. It computes true length only.
- Grounding, bonding, meter-main, service entrance cable, mast: only "conduit 2 in" and a meter socket exist.
- Whole-house: the 3D picture. trades/3-electrical.png shows no panel box, no device on the wall; I cannot find the 12 inches I need clearance of in front of the panel (NEC working space) or the 30 in width. No clearance rule for the panel.
- Inspector's check: no GFCI/AFCI field on a device, no kitchen/bath rule check, no spacing check against a drawn device (only the computed floor count).
- Colour: Power and Lighting both orange; I cannot tell a 15 A lighting from a 50 A range feeder.
- "Verify in field" for electrical shows size, but not the things I verify: panel brand/age (Federal Pacific, Zinsco), knob and tube, aluminum branch wiring, grounded/ungrounded. These are the reasons a renovation electrical price moves.
- The sheet's Electrical tab (25 lines) not seen.

## 4. What the best tools show today
- ConstructConnect Takeoff (electrical): linear measures of conduit and cable and counts of device symbols from drawings, with assemblies. https://www.constructconnect.com/products/constructconnect-takeoff/electrical
- Vendors claim wire and conduit quantities by tracing circuits panel to device with rises, offsets and service loops (marketing claims, unverified): see the roundup https://www.guideflow.com/blog/electrical-estimating-software and https://www.oncenter.com/sub-contractor/electrical-estimating-software/
- Panel schedule reading (poles, trips, loads): Drawer AI per the Fitgap listing https://us.fitgap.com/search/construction-estimating-software/electrical
- Assembly libraries for panel boards, lighting systems, conduit runs: Fitgap listing above. 3D/BIM takeoff: Autodesk Construction Cloud, RIB CostX per https://www.getapp.com/construction-software/takeoff/f/electrical-estimating
- Code: 2023 NEC 210.8(A) GFCI now for all kitchen receptacles; 210.52(C) counter receptacles not below the work surface; island/peninsula receptacle optional; AFCI areas in 210.12(B) (summaries, section numbers disagree between sources; verify against NFPA 70 text): https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/article/21262640/whats-cooking-in-the-kitchen-for-the-2023-nec and https://www.dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/NECFAQ23.pdf

Where this program could be ahead, not level: none of the above are 3D-first and renovation-first with the Assumed/Known idea. Ahead means: (a) the cable path is computed through the real house, so the drop in a wall, the home run through the attic and the bore through a joist are counted by what the model holds, not by a guessed factor; (b) existing wiring is Assumed until I open the box, and the dollars riding on each Assumed ride at the top of my list; (c) a wall marked to demolish tells me the circuits in it; (d) the room count turns into devices placed, with the inspector's rule named on each; (e) move a device and the cable length and the box fill change live.

## 5. My ten
1. Panel as a thing with a schedule. Click the panel: a panel card shows the make, bus amps, main, spaces total/used (each Assumed until I measure it), and a table of circuits: number, breaker A, poles, what it feeds (room), wire, AFCI/GFCI, load VA. A row "Service load, NEC 220 (optional method), 82 A of 100 A" with the arithmetic visible. Done when I can say "100 A is not enough" from the screen and the figure rests on Assumed loads, flagged.
2. Circuits are objects, not cable names. Draw a circuit from the panel (Cable tool picks a breaker) to devices; each circuit is coloured, listed, belongs to a room set, and carries gauge. Done when the takeoff reads "12/2 NM: 340 LF" and "14/2 NM: 410 LF" separately, with a breaker line for each.
3. Real cable and conduit types: 14/2, 12/2, 12/3, 10/3, 6/3, SE-R, THHN in EMT/PVC, by gauge; each priceable per LF with a reel/coil purchase unit. Done when a price list import can match "12/2 NM-B 250 ft coil".
4. Device library for a house: receptacle (15/20 A, TR, GFCI, AFCI), switch (1P, 3-way, 4-way, dimmer), smoke/CO, recessed light, surface light, fan, disconnect, subpanel, with box type attached (so each device brings its box, plate, wire nuts as lines). Done when each shows as a small symbol in 3D with See through, and the takeoff counts them by type.
5. Existing against new, in the electrical layer: existing wire/device drawn pale, to demolish red, new orange; a line "Existing circuits in work being demolished: 3, cap or reroute" listed on the wall; a tie-in point that is a connection, not a clash. Done when a demolished partition with a receptacle in it lists "1 receptacle, 12 LF cable, circuit 7" in the VIF list.
6. Room receptacle layout that is the inspector's: place the computed outlets as devices (wall spacing 12 ft rule, 6 ft rule on any wall space 2 ft or more), kitchen counters (every counter 2 ft or wider, 24 in rule), bath GFCI at the basin, hallway, outdoor, garage, with a check "spacing met" per room and the section named. Done when moving a door moves the outlets and the count; bath shows "1 GFCI at basin", not 3.
7. Cable route through the house: home run length from panel to first box, drop at each box, 8 in/6 in free conductor per box, service loop, and through-floor bored holes counted once per riser (a count of holes, not 15 clashes). Done when one circuit shows "home run 42 LF, drops 17 LF, loops 4 LF" and a click on a number shows what made it.
8. Cable against structure and other trades: joists/rafters/studs (the model has walls only), ducts, drains, a wall to be demolished, with the cable's allowable bore (inches from edge, 1 1/4 in rule). Done when a cable run through a drawn joist shows "bore hole, 2 in from edge, needs a plate" instead of nothing.
9. Rough, trim and labor: each device line carries labor hours I can override (rough, trim, hours per 100 LF of pull) and can be set to my own rate; total split material/labor like a sheet I use. Done when the electrical subcontract export lists labor hours by task.
10. Existing-condition prompts in VIF: for each existing panel/circuit, the questions I ask on a walk: panel brand and age, spaces, grounded/ungrounded, knob and tube, aluminum, box fill, junction in wall; with dollars riding. Done when the VIF sheet exported has those as boxes to tick.

## 6. What must not change
- The honesty: Assumed/Known/Unknown on every figure, "no figure" over a guess, and "fireplaces, fixed cabinets... not worked here" saying what the count leaves out.
- One building, my layer is a switch; See through gives a clean ghost of the house (trades/3, glass/08).
- Cable and conduit lengths agree with a hand sum (earlier review hand-checked 41 LF); the room 210.52 floor-count arithmetic was hand-checked and right.
- Clashes ordered by dollars, VIF export as a field sheet, hole-through-wall as a named kind.
- Demolish/existing marking and the guard that existing work is not bought again.
- US trade units and no software words.
