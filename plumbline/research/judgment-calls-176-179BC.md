# PIECE 1. What a wall carries and what it stands on (call 176)

Worst first. None of these is "safe"/engineer wording: every carries block says "worked from what is drawn above and below this line; it is not an engineer's finding". Nothing was found that reads as safe.

## 1-1. A wall above that only TOUCHES the wall below is said to stand on it for the whole length (wrong quantity, misleading)
Repro (empty job; `w` = wall 6 in): ground wall `0,0 24,0`; second-floor wall `0,0.5 24,0.5` (centre lines 6 in apart, so the two 6 in walls share one face and overlap by 0 in).
Said (carries second): `stands on: wall 1 on ground [wall-0007] (wall) for 24.00 LF`; ground: `carries: wall 1 on second ... for 24.00 LF`.
By hand: 0 in of bearing. Offset 5 in (1 in of the 6 in overlap) also said "for 24.00 LF". Offset 7 in (gap 1 in) says "nothing found under it ... ordinary for a partition". The cliff is at "centre-to-centre <= thickness". Offsets 0, 2, 5, 6 all print the identical sentence; the sentence never says how far off the wall is. A wall hanging 5/6 off its support reads exactly like one directly over it.

## 1-2. An angled wall above: all or nothing. 2 degrees = "nothing found" for the whole length though ~14 LF still lies over the wall below
Repro: ground `0,0 24,0`; second `0,0 24,0.838` (2.0 degrees, 10 in off at the far end). Said (second): `nothing found under it for 24.01 LF: it stands on the floor alone there, which is ordinary for a partition`; ground: `nothing found standing on it`.
By hand: its centre line stays within 6 in of the wall below for the first 14.3 ft. At 1 degree (`24,0.4189`) it says the full 24.00 LF. So a 1.0 degree wall carries 24 LF and a 2.0 degree one 0 LF. Also the length says 24.01 vs 24.00 (fine, hypotenuse).
Worse in the demolish case: a wall of the level above at 2 degrees on top of a wall to demolish: no caution, no question (the same rule).

## 1-3. "ordinary for a partition" said under a wall with a beam plainly under it
Repro: second wall `0,8 24,8` (marked existing lower wall not needed); ground beam on the line, two posts at its ends, two pads under them, beam `--top` varied.
Top 9 ft, 8 ft 2 in, 8 ft, 9 ft 6 in, 10 ft: `stands on: beam 1 [beam-0012] (beam) for 24.00 LF`. Top 7 ft 6 in (18 in below the base of the wall above; a dropped beam under the joists) and 7 ft: `nothing found under it for 24.00 LF: it stands on the floor alone there, which is ordinary for a partition` -- with a beam, two posts and two pads exactly in line below it. 1 ft is a hard threshold; nothing tells the person the beam was seen and ruled out.
Also (the other side): a beam whose top is 10 ft on the ground level (a foot ABOVE the floor the wall above stands on, i.e. through the floor) is still said to be what the wall stands on. Neither end is checked.
Also: posts and pads are never said. The beam line reads the same for a beam on two posts on pads as for a beam hung in the air. `structure` lists them, `carries` never connects them. (The builder said this; it still means the sentence about the beam carries the wall is the only thing a builder reads.)

## 1-4. A wall marked to demolish under a wall TWO levels up (an empty level between): no caution, no question
Repro: levels ground 0, mid 9 ft (no walls drawn, e.g. a floor-only or attic level), top 18 ft; wall on ground and wall over it on top; `mark <ground wall> demolish`.
Said (check): nothing. (`carries`: both "nothing found" and for the top wall `nothing found under it ... ordinary for a partition`.) The level above is "the next higher elevation" even when it holds no wall. With three real levels the chain works, but only one level is said ("wall 1 on mid stands on it").

## 1-7. A footing wider than the wall is "no footing" if the wall sits off its centre

# PIECE 3. The figures a judge found wrong, after their fix (call 179 C)

Helpers: rt.sh (export ifc, import ifc into an empty job, compare every wall's line/length/face/net/opening and every opening), walls.py, jh.py (independent joist count), rot scans in rotscan.txt.

## 3-1. A floor a hair out of square loses a joist (wrong quantity, silent, changes with the 4th decimal)
Repro (empty job; level ground; floor type 10 in; line "Floor joists, 2x10 at 16 in" = joists):
`floor ... --points "0,0 24,0 24,16 0,16"` -> 19 joists, 304 LF (hand 24/1.3333 + 1 = 19).
`--points "0,0 24,0.0001 24,16 0,16"` (the south side off by 0.0001 ft = 1/80 in over 24 ft) -> **18 joists, 287.9991 LF**. Same for 24,0.001 and 24,0.01; for `24,0 24.0001,16` (east side 0.0001 ft out) and for `23.9999,0`. A west-side shift of 0.0001 ft leaves 19. The same rectangle rotated 1, 3, 5, 7 ... 83 degrees with its corners given to 4 decimals comes out one joist short at every spacing (12: 24 for 25; 16: 18 for 19; 19.2: 15 for 16; 24: 12 for 13); rotated to 5 decimals (45 degrees) or exactly (the 3-4-5 rotation) 19. So a traced floor, which is never exact, loses its edge joist whenever the two long edges are not parallel to the last digit. A traced trapezoid (24.013,0.021 / 23.987,16.004 / 0.008,15.99) 18, a 24.2/24.1 one 19.
By hand the count is 19, 304 LF; the program is one 16 LF joist short (small money) but the figure is not stable to anything a person could see.

## 3-2. A rim along an angled edge is counted by its plan projection, not its length
Repro: floor `0,0 24,0 24,16 10,16 0,6` (a 10 ft x 10 ft chamfer at the north-west corner), same line plus `rim_length`: said "rim length 48 LF" (24 south + 14 north + 10). (Joists themselves match hand figures: 19 and 261.8333 / 287.6458 LF by an independent chord count.)

## 3-3. IFC: a stub of wall shorter than its own thickness comes back turned 90 degrees, and can take a real wall down with it
Repro: wall 6 in thick `10,10 10.3333,10` (4 in long) and a 20 ft wall `0,0 20,0`; `export ifc`; `import ifc` into an empty job. Said: "1 wall ... not brought in: 1 thing / 1 wall: this program would not take it: there is already something called "wall 6 in" in the job: two things in one place cannot go by one name". Result: the stub is read as (10.1666, 9.75)-(10.1666, 10.25) (0.5 ft long, 4.5 SF where 3 SF was drawn), thickness 4 in but the type is still named "wall 6 in" (the file's own name for it), and the **20 ft wall is the one refused**, so 180 SF of wall is gone from the read-back. ## 3-4. IFC: a level's height does not come back (changed number, said nowhere)
Repro: levels basement (-8 ft 4 in, height 8 ft 4 in), "main floor" (0, height 9 ft 1 1/8 in), "upper floor" (9 ft 7 1/2 in, height 8 ft); walls on each; export and import with `--status Known`. Read-back: main floor height **9.625 ft** (the distance to the next level, i.e. height plus the floor depth, 6 3/8 in more than drawn), basement 8.3333 as drawn, upper floor height Unknown ("the model has no level above this one to say how tall it is"). The 9.625 is Known on the strength of the --status given, sourced to the file. The export's own list says everything entered goes with it; the import's list of what it did not bring in says nothing of level heights. Wall heights, drawn lengths, face areas (to the 4th decimal: 166.6667 -> 166.666 SF, 100 -> 99.9996 SF), openings and sills (2' 8 1/2" -> 2.7083 ft) all came back; only a level height changed, and that value is used where a wall or room takes its height from the level.

## 3-5. Siding over a stone wainscot: held. One rule to know.
40 ft x 10 ft wall, one 3 ft x 4 ft window, `apply` the starter "Exterior wall, 2x6, lap siding over a stone wainscot" (wainscot = 3 ft): sills 0 / 6 in / 2 ft 11 in / 3 ft / 3 ft 1 in give siding 277 / 275.5 / 268.25 / 268 / 268 SF, each equal to 280 less the window area above 3 ft (3, 4.5, 11.75, 12, 12), net area 388 SF all; the stone line 37 LF with the sill at 0 and 40 LF otherwise. Right by the rule. The rule: the stone is priced by LF on the whole wall length except where an opening comes to the floor, so a window with a sill of 6 in (7.5 SF of it within the 3 ft band) still buys the full 40 LF x 3 ft = 120 SF of stone, 7.5 SF more than is laid. Doors at either end of a wall (offset 1.5 ft and 28.5 ft on a 30 ft wall, 3 ft doors) are accepted.

## 3-6. Openings: a door or window that goes above the wall top is accepted, and the wall's net area becomes Unknown with no reason said
Repro: 30 ft wall 9 ft high; window 3 x 4 ft at sill 8 ft (top 12 ft); or a 12 ft door. Accepted. `show`: "net area unknown / opening area unknown"; `takeoff`: "wall face, net  no total, 1 of 1 missing"; `check`: "reads, its figures agree with what is drawn"; `vif`: nothing. It does not say that an opening is taller than the wall. A negative sill is refused with a clear sentence. Openings against openings: two 3 ft doors with 1 in between (offsets 5 ft and 8 ft 1 in) accepted; a second door overlapping the first by 0.01 ft refused with "an opening is clear of every other opening in its wall"; a window with sill 7 ft over a 6 ft 8 in door in the same place accepted; a window with sill 6 ft over the door refused naming the door; the same house's door at offset 1.4999 ft (it overlaps the 1.5 ft door I had put there, so the refusal is right).

## 3-7. Refusals: partly overlapping beams and pads are not refused
Same line, same top, same ends: refused ("two beams in one place would be counted twice"). A beam 5 ft-15 ft on a line already holding 0-10 and 10-20 (same top 9 ft): accepted; `structure` lists Beam 1, 2 and 3, 10 LF each, 30 LF over a 20 ft run. A beam 1 in off the line (3 1/2 in wide, so they overlap) is accepted. A pad footing 24 in x 24 in set 1 ft from another (they overlap by half) is accepted; only the identical point is refused. A post 3.4 in from another post (3 1/2 in posts) is refused, 3.5 in accepted; so posts are tested for overlap and pads are not. Floors: sharing an edge accepted (right), touching at a corner accepted (right), a floor 1 in into another refused, one inside another refused, the same outline reversed refused, the same outline on another level accepted. Floors cannot be given a hole (a stair well): an outline that returns to itself is refused with "the outline has to be a simple loop" ("simple loop" is not a builder's word), and nothing offers a way to leave an opening in a floor.

## 3-8. Holes and laid in
Held: a pipe through a 6 in wall at 90, 30 and 5 degrees: 1 hole each; at 30 degrees "at (8.7321, 0, 4)" = the hand figure (7 + 6 x 0.288675 = 8.7321); a pipe at 1 degree that never leaves the wall's thickness: 1 laid in, 0 holes; a pipe along the wall's middle or 0.2 ft off it: laid in; a pipe 0.4 ft off the centre line (skin 0.8 in off the face): neither (right); a pipe from outside ending in the wall: 1 hole; an upright pipe wholly inside a wall: laid in at its middle (10, 0, 4.5). Floors: a riser from -2 to 3 ft, from -0.5 to 2 ft, -2 to -0.4 ft: one hole each; wholly inside the 10 in slab: laid in; resting on the slab top or hanging from its bottom face: nothing.
Not right: (a) the place printed for a pipe laid in a slab is sometimes not on its middle: the same 10 ft pipe at -0.2 ft depth in a 30 x 10 ft slab: "(3, 3, -0.2)" at y=3 but "(7, 2, -0.1417)" at y=2 (x=2..8: its middle is 3, and z -0.1417 is not the pipe's depth); "(0.5385, 2, -0.1417)" for a 22 ft one. The place is where a builder walks to. (b) A pipe that starts in the slab and runs on past its edge (20,2 to 30,2, slab to 25) is laid in, the 5 ft outside the slab being ignored (depth only is tested). (c) a run that starts inside the wall and leaves it through a face at a shallow angle (0.1 ft to 0.35 ft across a wall whose face is at 0.25): 1 hole, right.
