# The visual standard

What every screen of plumbline is held to. It is written so that a person, or a judge
who has never seen the program, can look at one picture and say pass or fail on each
line. The rule it serves (CLAUDE.md): **the look is an instrument**, and **the 3D house
looks like the house**. DECISIONS.md calls 55 (the screen) and 58 (the dressed house)
have the reasons.

A picture fails a line if any one place in it breaks that line. "Not in this picture"
is a third answer, not a pass.

## A. The frame: both windows

1. **Flat.** Panels, bars and buttons are flat fills separated by hairlines. No drop
   shadows, no gradients, no glow, no rounded "pill" buttons, no decorative icons.
2. **Dense, and nothing cut.** Rows are tight (a row of a list or a table is under
   about 30 px tall). No text is clipped, cut by an edge, written over other text, or
   ended in an ellipsis where the room to show it exists. Nothing overlaps.
3. **Columns line up.** Figures in a column are right-aligned to one edge, with their
   units after them; names are left-aligned to one edge.
4. **One way to say how sure.** Known is plain ink with no mark; Assumed is amber;
   Unknown is red; and a figure's color and its small square marker agree. No other
   use is made of amber or red.
5. **One accent.** One blue marks what is selected or active, and is used for nothing
   else.
6. **A builder's words.** Every word is one a builder or an estimator says. Units are
   written as the trades write them: SF, LF, EA, SQ, CY, LS, LB, hr, sheets. No
   software term, no file or program jargon, no key or identifier shown where a name
   belongs.
7. **It says what to do next and what just happened.** The status line along the
   bottom is whole and readable; a refusal says why and what to do instead.

## B. The plan

1. **It reads as a drawing.** The floor is paper. Walls are drawn in ink as their two
   faces and their ends. Openings read as openings in their walls. What is overhead
   (the roof over a level) is drawn in broken lines.
2. **Lines by type.** Each kind of roof line (eave, rake, ridge, hip, valley) has its
   one color everywhere it is drawn or named, and a line with no length is broken and
   red.
3. **A drawing laid under the tracing** is shown as it is, flat and unlit, and the
   tracing reads against it.
4. **Runs** (duct, pipe, conduit, cable) are in their system's color at their own
   size, and read apart from the walls.

## C. The model in 3D

1. **Shaded under studio light:** one light that casts shadows, a weaker fill, and
   light from all round. Shadows are soft and never black.
2. **A neutral ground** with a faint grid and no horizon line. **No sky, no sun, no
   grass, no trees, no backdrop.**
3. **Matte.** No surface is glossy. Glass may reflect a little; bare metal may be a
   satin. Nothing has a hot white highlight.
4. **Still.** Nothing flickers, shimmers or swims between two pictures of the same
   view; no two surfaces fight for the same place (speckle or stripes where they meet).
5. **What is selected** is outlined in the accent and keeps the color of what it is
   made of.

## D. The house, dressed

1. **Dressed in what is priced, bare where nothing is.** A wall, a roof plane, a
   window or a door with a finish priced on it is drawn in that finish; one with none
   is a plain flat color. The Finish block of the panel says which, and what line says
   so, and agrees with the picture.
2. **At size and level.** Courses of siding, brick, block, stone, shingles and slate
   run level; seams and battens run plumb or straight up the slope; a brick, a lap and
   a seam are their real sizes against a door (a door is 6 ft 8 in to 7 ft tall: about
   30 brick courses, about 14 laps at 6 in).
3. **Corners close.** Two walls meet at a corner with no gap, notch or sliver; siding
   stops at a corner board; masonry turns the corner.
4. **Openings are built.** A window has a frame, a sash and glass, and casing and a
   sill outside; a hung window has two sashes; divided lites have their bars. A door
   has a frame and a leaf; a panel door has its stiles, rails and panels; a knob. All
   of it sits in the wall, none of it floats in front of it or is cut by it.
5. **The roof sits on the house.** Its edge has a fascia; where it overhangs a wall
   the underside is closed (a soffit), not open to the inside; a gutter is drawn only
   where one is priced.
6. **It looks like a house a builder would build**, at the standard of good
   residential work: proportions, trim and materials a builder would recognize. A
   picture that reads as a toy, a diagram of boxes, or a game fails this line.

## E. The sheet

1. **It reads as an estimate.** The recap first (cost types, job cost, overhead,
   profit, price), then the cost by division, all sixteen in order, then every line
   under its division's heading, then the suppliers side by side.
2. **Every total says how sure it is and what it leaves out.** A total that leaves a
   line out is marked and names the line. "none" means no work; "no figure" means work
   with no price yet.
3. **What can be typed is a field on its own line:** a unit cost, a stretch-out, a
   division, overhead and profit percent. A field looks like a field.

## How a picture is judged

For each picture: the lines of A apply to all; B to a plan view; C and D to a 3D view;
E to the sheet window. The judge writes, per picture, each line as pass, fail (with
the place in the picture and what is wrong), or not in this picture. A screen passes
when no line fails on any picture of it.
